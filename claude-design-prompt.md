# Claude Design prompt — Equestrian Coaches Australia (5 directions)

Copy everything below the line into Claude Design as one prompt.

---

Design 5 distinct visual directions for the homepage of a new website. I'm choosing a direction, not a finished build — show me hero plus one content section per direction, not full pages.

BUSINESS
Equestrian Coaches Australia is a new online directory connecting riders with riding coaches across Australia. Riders search free, by discipline and location, to find the right coach nearby. Coaches pay a monthly subscription ($9.99 for a standard profile — bio, photo, location, specialties, qualifications, testimonials; $14.95 to also list clinics and events) to be listed and found. Launch categories include Western, Dressage and Liberty, with more disciplines to follow. It's a pre-launch venture with no existing brand yet — this exploration is where the brand starts.

AUDIENCE
Two groups, one homepage. Riders: casual, browsing free, deciding whether a coach looks right for them — credible, warm, easy to scan by discipline and location. Coaches: professionals who need the site to look credible enough to pay a monthly fee to be listed on. Skews adult, broad age range, majority female, recreational-through-competitive riders across Australia.

OBJECTIVE
Two conversions from one page: get a rider to start a discipline + location search, and get a coach to start signing up to list their profile. Neither should bury the other.

THE FEELING
Premium and editorial, but warm and community-led — not cold or corporate. It should feel like it belongs to real coaches and real riders, while looking considered enough that a coach is comfortable paying to be listed next to their name.

VISUAL TERRITORY
The closest functional comparison the client gave me is Horse Deals (horsedeals.com.au), Australia's long-running equestrian classifieds marketplace. Screenshots show its actual territory: a serif wordmark paired with a bold, rounded-terminal sans for every other headline and UI label, both set in a soft navy-charcoal rather than true black; a single warm coral-red accent (roughly #E2604F) carries every call-to-action and promo link against a cream-white ground; photography is candid, naturally lit, documentary-style equestrian imagery with no colour grading; corners carry a small uniform radius on both buttons and cards, never a pill; and the layout alternates an airy full-bleed hero with a tight, information-dense classifieds grid built for scanning volume. It reads as a practical, high-utility rural-marketplace aesthetic — warm and unpretentious, but not premium or aspirational. The card density and plain grey-bordered tiles signal "this is where you get things done," not "this is a considered brand."

Treat Horse Deals as a functional floor only — the category it proves out (a trusted, homegrown, unmistakably Australian equestrian service marketplace) — not a visual target. Do not carry over its dense classifieds-grid card style, its grey-bordered thumbnail tiles, or its coral-on-navy accent system as-is. All five directions should look and feel more considered, more editorial, and more intentional than that reference, while still feeling like they belong to the same honest, community-rooted world rather than a generic SaaS template.

All five directions live in this territory as five different interpretations of it, not five layouts of one design. Each has its own device, palette and type pairing.

THE FIVE DIRECTIONS

Direction A — The Paddock Edit
Argument: warm editorial magazine energy — the site reads like a considered publication about the sport, not a form to fill in.
Device: a soft, single-stroke arch (echoing an arena entrance or stable doorway) — as a large low-opacity watermark behind the hero copy, as a framing element around the featured-coach photograph, and as a small accent above section headings.
Palette: cream ground, deep hunter-green dark, terracotta accent, warm sand tint.
Type: warm humanist serif for display, clean grotesque sans for UI and body.
Structure: asymmetric hero — large portrait-oriented photograph of a coach mid-lesson to one side, headline and search stacked to the other. Generous whitespace.
Photography: warm, naturally graded, shot low and close — riders and coaches mid-lesson, not posed.
Density: generous, editorial pacing.

Direction B — The Riding Register
Argument: the warmest and most approachable — a friendly community directory that happens to look sharp.
Device: a simple ribbon-fold badge shape (a rectangle with a small triangular notch cut from one edge, never a literal rosette) — behind discipline tags, at large scale as a photo-corner accent, and as a small marker on coach profile cards.
Palette: warm sand/cream ground, saddle-tan dark, deep navy accent, soft clay tint.
Type: rounded-but-grown-up sans for both display and UI (single family, two weights doing the display/body work).
Structure: search-first hero — discipline and location fields sit directly in the hero, not below it. Coach profile cards are the hero's centre of gravity.
Photography: candid, warm, community-feel — coaches and riders together, genuine expressions.
Density: medium, friendly, easy to scan.

Direction C — Field Notes
Argument: the most restrained and premium — heritage equestrian club energy, quietly confident rather than loud.
Device: a single fine stitched-seam rule (echoing saddle leather stitching) — as a divider under the nav, as a border treatment on the featured coach card, and as an underline accent on the active discipline filter.
Palette: deep forest-navy dark, warm ivory ground, muted brass accent, pale sage tint.
Type: refined editorial serif for display, a quiet grotesque sans for UI, with a light monospace-flavoured numeral style for prices/distances only.
Structure: centred, classical hero with a tight vertical rhythm; content section uses a structured grid of coach cards with a strong shared baseline.
Photography: composed rather than candid — clean, well-lit arena and competition shots.
Density: tight, considered, premium.

Direction D — The Local Ring
Argument: leads with the thing riders actually came to do — find someone near them.
Device: a simple concentric-ring mark (echoing a dressage arena viewed from above, not a literal map pin) — as a background texture behind the location search, at large scale intersecting the hero photo's edge, and as a small radius indicator on coach cards ("12km away").
Palette: warm cream ground, charcoal dark, coral-orange accent (evolved warmer and more saturated than Horse Deals' coral, paired with better contrast and used more sparingly), soft eucalyptus tint.
Type: confident grotesque sans for display, same family lighter weight for body.
Structure: hero built around the search bar itself — discipline and location fields large and central, photography framing rather than filling the hero.
Photography: candid, outdoors, Australian rural settings — paddocks, arenas, western landscapes.
Density: medium, functional-but-warm.

Direction E — Blue Ribbon
Argument: the direction that takes a real position — bolder, more saturated, more fashion-editorial than the brief strictly asks for. This one pushes further; don't soften it toward the others.
Device: a bold diagonal sash/band shape (a geometric colour-blocked stripe, not a literal ribbon graphic) — as a large diagonal field behind the hero headline, wrapped around a corner of the featured photograph, and as a small tag-shaped accent on discipline labels.
Palette: saturated navy-black dark, warm white ground, a confident single saturated accent (deep berry-red or cobalt — pick one and commit), gold-brass tint used sparingly as a third note.
Type: large-scale bold display serif with dramatic size contrast against a plain grotesque sans for everything else.
Structure: asymmetric, full-bleed, dramatic crops — the hero photograph bleeds off two edges, headline overlaps the sash device.
Photography: bolder crops, higher contrast, competition-moment energy (mid-jump, mid-transition) rather than calm portraiture.
Density: confident, a little tighter than A or C, built for impact over browsing.

TYPOGRAPHY STANDARDS
Never use Inter, Poppins, Montserrat, Roboto, Open Sans, Lato, Raleway, Nunito, or Source Sans, in any direction.
- Directions A, C, E need an editorial/display serif category: think in the spirit of Canela, Tiempos, or Freight Text. Use Fraunces or Source Serif 4 as the Google Fonts fallback.
- Directions B, D, and the UI/body layer of A, C, E need a grotesque/humanist sans category: think in the spirit of Söhne, Graphik, or National. Use Work Sans or Public Sans as the Google Fonts fallback.
- Two font families maximum per direction.
- Scale contrast at least 5x between the largest display size and body text.
- Body text minimum 17px (18px for any block aimed at the coach-signup flow, since that audience skews slightly older and needs to read terms comfortably).

IMAGERY
Real placeholder photographs only — Unsplash or picsum, never grey boxes, colour swatches or gradients standing in for a photo. Subjects needed: a coach actively teaching a lesson (for the hero, portrait orientation, crop so the coach and rider both read clearly with headroom for overlaid type in directions where copy sits on the image); a relaxed candid shot of a rider and coach together post-lesson (content section); at least one western-discipline and one dressage-discipline shot so the "search by discipline" idea reads visually, not just in copy; an arena or outdoor Australian rural setting as a texture/background shot for directions B and D. Note the crop for each so it doubles as the real photographer's brief later.

PROTECTED ZONES
In any photograph containing a person, the face and the area immediately around it are protected. No decorative element, device instance, colour panel, badge or text block may overlap:
- The subject's face
- The area within roughly 15% of the image height around the face
- Any hand or object the subject is holding, if it carries meaning

This must hold at 1440, 768 AND 375. Compositions reflow at mobile, and that is where the overlap usually appears.

Before returning anything, state for each direction and each breakpoint where the device sits relative to the subject, and confirm the face is clear. If a device instance cannot clear the face at a breakpoint, move it or drop that instance at that breakpoint and say which you chose.

COMMON FAILURES — DO NOT PRODUCE THESE
- Buttons use a single radius value across all four corners. If a button includes a circular badge or icon container, the badge diameter equals the button height and the button's radius equals half its height, so the two forms read as one object. Never mix a rounded end with a square end on the same element.
- No decorative shape may cross a subject's face at any breakpoint (see Protected Zones).
- Copy placed over photography always sits on a gradient, panel, or blur — never raw image behind text.
- Mobile reorganises the layout; it does not just stack desktop columns into one column.
- Avoid generic marketplace/directory furniture unless a direction specifically calls for it: no three-icon "how it works" row, no centred testimonial carousel, no numbered process stepper, no "as seen in" logo strip, no literal rosette-ribbon clipart, no generic teardrop map-pin icon, no stock handshake photography, no faux-handwritten "equestrian" script fonts.
- Spacing values come from a consistent scale — nothing arbitrary or eyeballed.
- Nothing sits within 4px of a viewport edge without a deliberate reason; either bleed fully or respect the gutter.

REFERENCES
Horse Deals — horsedeals.com.au — functional floor described above (classifieds-marketplace mechanics, Australian equestrian trust signals). Elevate the style; do not copy the visual system.

CONSTRAINTS / DO NOT / HERO COPY
No existing logo, brand colours or fonts — this exploration defines them. Do not invent statistics, coach counts, testimonials or credentials; where the layout needs a number (e.g. "500+ coaches"), mark it clearly as a placeholder and list every one at the end. This is draft copy — treat it as a placeholder to be refined, not final:
- Headline: "Find your perfect riding coach, nearby."
- Subhead: "Search verified coaches across Australia by discipline and location — dressage, western, liberty and more."
- Primary CTA (rider path): "Find a coach"
- Secondary CTA (coach path): "List your coaching profile"

PRESENTATION FORMAT
This gets exported to PDF for the client, so:
- Artboard width 1600px, so full-width sections render sharp
- Each direction starts a new page: page-break-before: always on each direction block
- page-break-inside: avoid on every image and every card
- Nothing relies on hover or scroll to be understood
- A cover block: "Equestrian Coaches Australia", "Homepage — five directions", today's date
- A closing block: what happens next and the feedback deadline
- Backgrounds render in print: use -webkit-print-color-adjust: exact
- Do not place content in the outer 40px of any page

Two toggles in the Tweaks panel:
- showSpecs (default off) — hex values, font names, sizes, spacing, device geometry, ratios. This is studio data, not for the client view.
- showMobile (default on) — the 375 compositions.

Each direction in the client view gets, in this order: a full-width divider carrying the direction letter and name; one plain-language sentence describing the feel (no jargon, no colour or typeface names); the desktop hero at 1440; the content section at 1440; the mobile composition at 375 in a device-width frame.

PALETTE SWITCHER
Give each direction three palette options as clickable swatch trios beneath the composition. Clicking one re-themes that direction live.
- Implement by swapping CSS custom property values on a wrapper element. Do not duplicate the markup.
- Each palette keeps the same roles: dark, accent, tint, ground. Only the values change.
- All three must pass WCAG 2.2 AA in every text pairing used on the page. Do not offer a palette that fails; check before including it.
- Label them in plain language, not colour theory: e.g. "Warmer," "Cooler," "Higher contrast."
- Palette 1 is the recommended one and loads by default.
- Switching affects only that direction, not the whole page.
- In the PDF export, render each direction in its default palette and show the alternates as static swatch rows.

DELIVERABLE
Five directions, one per mini-brief above. Each: hero plus ONE content section (the discipline + location search moment, since that's the core of the product), at 1440 and 375.

Do not build full pages. I am choosing a direction, not a layout.

Per direction, in the studio view only (showSpecs on):
- Colour and type, hex and font names
- The device and its three instances
- How mobile reorganises
- What each placeholder image stands in for
- Confirmation that no element overlaps a face at any breakpoint

Before starting, raise anything genuinely ambiguous.

FINAL CHECKS
1. Would this work for another business with the logo swapped?
2. Describable without "layout," "split," "stacked," "overlaid"?
3. Any element with mixed corner radii?
4. Any decoration over a face at any breakpoint?
