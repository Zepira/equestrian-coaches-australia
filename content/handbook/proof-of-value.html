Worth Paying For

How we measure our own reach, and how we prove to a coach that the platform earned its keep — before we ask any of them for money.

Build spec Ships before coach profile #1 Supabase · Search Console · Resend September 2026

Two different jobs get confused here. Reach tells Kim and Alana where the budget goes. Value is what a coach will pay for, and it is a completely different number. Everything below is built backwards from one moment: the month-eight email that asks a founding coach for real money.

Cannot be added later Day 1

Events can't be backfilled. Every week without logging is a week of evidence gone, and it's the evidence we need most.

Everything serves this Month 8

The founding coaches move from free to paid. Whatever makes that email work is what we instrument now.

The strongest early number Google

Search Console impressions, per coach profile. Third-party, free, and it works before we have any traffic of our own.

01

What we are proving, and to whom

Page views are ours. Enquiries are theirs. A coach who is shown a view count and asked for $19 will conclude, correctly, that we are charging them for a number they cannot check.

What a coach actually believes, weakest to strongest
 The claimWhy it lands, or doesn't
1 "Your profile was viewed 47 times" Weakest. Unverifiable, and it reads like a number invented to justify an invoice.
2 "You appeared in Google search results 89 times" Much stronger, because it is Google's number rather than ours. Free, and it works in month three when we have almost no traffic. No other Australian equine directory shows a coach this.
3 "12 people tapped to see your phone number" Real intent, and hard to wave away. This is the number the contact design exists to capture.
4 "3 riders sent you an enquiry — here they are" Named, timestamped, undeniable. The one that carries the month-eight email.
5 "You told us 2 of them booked" The closer. It only exists if we ask, so we ask — one click, every month.
The risk that kills directories

A rider finds a coach here, texts them directly, books a lesson — and the coach never knows where it came from. Six months of that and they cancel, honestly believing they got nothing. Attribution isn't a reporting nicety; it is the product.

Why not just print the number

A phone number rendered straight onto the page is invisible to us. One extra tap converts an untrackable page view into a recorded signal of intent, and riders are entirely used to it. This is the single highest-value instrumentation decision in the build.

The mention prompt

Shown under the contact buttons and repeated in the enquiry confirmation. It costs nothing and it is the only thing that closes the loop on the phone path.

Use the coach's first name, never "the coach". Keep it a sentence, not a banner — it should read as a favour to Jane rather than a request from us.

03

The event log

One append-only table in Postgres, written server-side. Not GA4 — GA4 is for aggregate campaign attribution and cannot answer "which coach, which rider, when" in a way we can query for an email.

-- everything a rider does, in one place
create type public.event_kind as enum (
  'search_performed','result_impression','result_click',
  'profile_view','contact_reveal','enquiry_sent',
  'clinic_view','clinic_enquiry','share_click','referral_signup'
);

create type public.contact_channel as enum ('phone','email','website','facebook','instagram');

create table public.events (
  id             bigint generated always as identity primary key,
  kind           public.event_kind  not null,
  occurred_at    timestamptz not null default now(),
  coach_id       uuid references public.coaches(id) on delete cascade,
  area_id        uuid references public.areas(id),
  term_id        uuid references public.terms(id),   -- discipline or skill
  channel        public.contact_channel,
  position       int,                              -- rank in the result list
  query          text,                             -- what the rider typed
  source         text,                             -- utm_source, or 'organic' / 'direct'
  campaign       text,                             -- utm_campaign
  referrer_host  text,
  session_hash   text,                             -- salted DAILY, deliberately not stable
  device_class   text,                             -- mobile | desktop | bot
  meta           jsonb not null default '{}'
);

create index events_coach_time on public.events (coach_id, occurred_at desc);
create index events_kind_time  on public.events (kind, occurred_at desc);
create index events_campaign   on public.events (campaign) where campaign is not null;
Two decisions inside that schema

position on impressions and clicks. It's what lets us tell a coach "you appeared in 140 searches and were opened 12 times", and it's how we spot coaches who are consistently being scrolled past.

session_hash salted daily, not monthly. Enough to de-duplicate a single visitor refreshing a page; not enough to follow anyone around. Cheaper than a consent banner and a better answer to a rider who asks.

Two fields on the coach record, from day one

cohort (founding_25 / founding_100 / open) and acquisition_source. Different offers convert differently, and if we can't split them at month eight we will misread the only result that matters.

04

Enquiries, and whether they turned into anything

Enquiries get their own table — they hold personal information and they have a lifecycle, so they don't belong in an append-only log.

create table public.enquiries (
  id                uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  coach_id          uuid not null references public.coaches(id) on delete cascade,
  created_at        timestamptz not null default now(),
  rider_name        text not null,
  rider_email       text,
  rider_phone       text,
  rider_suburb      text,
  term_id           uuid references public.terms(id),
  message           text not null,
  source            text,
  notified_at       timestamptz,      -- when we emailed the coach
  opened_at         timestamptz,
  outcome           text check (outcome in ('booked','replied','not_suitable','no_response')),
  outcome_at        timestamptz
);

The outcome column is the only place ground truth lives. We get it by asking, once a month, with one click in the email — not by building a booking system.

Why the outcome question earns its place

It converts our claim into their claim. At month eight the email doesn't say "we sent you three enquiries" — it says "you told us two of these turned into lessons". That is a much harder thing to argue with, and it is the sentence that closes the sale.

It also gives us the only honest aggregate figure for recruiting cohort two: coaches who reported at least one booking, as a share of all paying coaches. Never quote it until it is real.

05

Google Search Console

A weekly cron pulling the Search Analytics API into Postgres. This is the most undervalued piece of the whole spec, because coach profile pages are pages — so Google's impression data attributes to individual coaches for free.

create table public.gsc_daily (
  date         date not null,
  page         text not null,
  query        text,
  device       text,
  impressions  int not null,
  clicks       int not null,
  position     numeric(4,1),
  primary key (date, page, query, device)
);

-- map a profile URL back to the coach it belongs to
create index gsc_page on public.gsc_daily (page, date desc);

Service account, webmasters.readonly, one row per date/page/query/device. Google holds roughly sixteen months, so a weekly pull is plenty — but start it on day one, because you cannot query what was never verified.

The same query data does three jobs

It tells a coach what riders near them are searching for. It tells us which area pages are close to page one and worth writing properly. And it feeds the search-gap report already spec'd in How Riders Find Us — which is also Kim's best cold-outreach line.

06

The monthly email

This ships before the dashboard. A dashboard is something a coach has to remember to visit; the email arrives whether they remember us or not, and it is the entire retention mechanism for months three to eight.

A nightly job rolls events into coach_month_stats, one row per coach per month. Freeze it — never recompute a past month from raw events, because if last month's number moves, every number we've ever sent becomes suspect.

create table public.coach_month_stats (
  coach_id            uuid not null references public.coaches(id) on delete cascade,
  month               date not null,          -- first of month
  result_impressions  int not null default 0, -- appeared in OUR search results
  result_clicks       int not null default 0,
  profile_views       int not null default 0,
  contact_reveals     int not null default 0,
  enquiries           int not null default 0,
  bookings_reported   int not null default 0,
  google_impressions  int not null default 0, -- from gsc_daily
  google_clicks       int not null default 0,
  google_position     numeric(4,1),
  area_median_views   int,                     -- benchmark, needs 10+ coaches nearby
  area_median_enq     int,
  frozen_at           timestamptz not null default now(),
  primary key (coach_id, month)
);

The rule the email is built on

Every month, give a coach something they cannot get anywhere else. Search terms near them, a benchmark against comparable coaches, and one specific gap in their profile — never a generic tip.

And lead with the strongest honest number available. In a zero month that is the Google figure, because it is still true and it is still Google's.

What the marketing dashboard shows

Rider sessions by source, coach signups by source, cost per coach and cost per rider by channel, and progress against the three checkpoint targets. Nothing else. It is read once a month, not watched.

09

Build order

Strictly in this sequence. The first two are the ones with a deadline attached, because they can't be backfilled.

1

The events table and a logging helper

Before coach profile number one goes live. A single server-side logEvent() called from the page handlers, with bot filtering and self-view exclusion built in from the start rather than added when the numbers look odd.

2

Contact block and enquiry form

Reveal buttons, the enquiry form, the mention prompt, and the coach notification email. This is the product decision that makes everything downstream possible.

3

Search Console verification and the weekly pull

Verify the domain the day it resolves. The ingest job can come later; the verification cannot, because Google only backfills from the day it knows you exist.

4

The nightly rollup

coach_month_stats, frozen at month end. Benchmarks switch on once an area has ten or more coaches; until then the column stays null and the email simply omits that line.

5

The monthly email

Both variants, with the outcome question. Send the first one to five coaches by hand and read their replies before automating it.

6

The coach dashboard, then ours

Theirs first, because it carries the conversion. Ours can be a Supabase view and a saved query for a long time before it needs to be a page.

Deliberately not building

Real-time dashboards, cohort retention curves, funnel charts, session recording, or a paid analytics tool. At a hundred coaches a nightly aggregation and a monthly email cover almost all of the value. GA4's free tier handles campaign attribution; revisit Plausible when there is revenue to spend.

10

Honesty, and the rules that protect it

In a community this small, one coach posting "their stats are made up" in one Facebook group costs more than the entire first-year ad budget. These are cheap now and unrecoverable later.

  • Filter bots before counting, not afterA coach shown 89 views that were all crawlers will find out. User-agent classification on write, and exclude anything flagged from every number we ever display.
  • Don't count a coach viewing their own profileOr us viewing anything. Both are trivially easy to exclude and embarrassing to explain later.
  • Freeze each month when it closesNever recompute history. A number that moves after it was sent poisons every number that follows it.
  • Never round up, and never estimate silentlyIf the benchmark needs ten coaches and an area has six, omit the line. Missing is fine; invented is not.
  • Say what a number does not mean"Appeared in Google" is not "someone read your profile", and the email should say so. Coaches trust the numbers more when we mark their limits.
  • A privacy policy before the first enquiry formWe're storing riders' names, suburbs and contact details. The Privacy Act's small business exemption applies under $3m turnover, but the policy costs an afternoon and the alternative is answering it badly in public.
  • Delete enquiry contact details after 24 monthsKeep the counts forever, keep the personal details only while they're useful. One scheduled job.
