Handbook Equine Professionals Australia

The site as a CMS

Every profession launches together, coaches and all eight horse care professions, so the list of professions, everything written about each one, and the way a farrier signs up can no longer live in the code. This is the plan for how the site runs from the admin instead: what is a row and what stays code, the database built clean from the start, how a professional signs up and gets reviewed, how riders are kept coming back across every profession, how value is proved to every provider, the admin screens, and the order to build it in.

Build plan, revision 2 24 September 2026 Decisions settled, section 13 Builds on One Site, Two Front Doors
01

The rule, in one line

The code owns the shape of the site. The database owns everything a visitor reads. Colours, layouts, the search algorithm, security and the page templates stay in code, because a wrong value there breaks the site. The professions, their names and words, the specialities, the page copy, the prices, the menus and the emails move into rows that Alana and Kim edit in /admin, because they will change without the code changing.

It is the settings rule from 7 September grown up. That rule already covers numbers and toggles. This extends it to words and to the taxonomy, with the same five protections (code defaults, typed readers, validation on save, a history row on every change, a short cache), which is what stops a CMS from becoming a way to break the site from a web form.

Not a page builder

Admin edits fields, never layout. A hero has a headline, a lead, a short lead and a photo, and those four fields are what the form shows. Nobody can drag a block onto the home page. That keeps the design intact whoever is typing, and it is also far less to build.

04

The data model

A profession is a terms row, its words live in a companion table, specialities hang off it, and a provider's professions are rows in provider_terms like their disciplines already are. The one new idea is that a profile is not an account: that is what keeps practices with several people a later addition rather than a rebuild.

A

Professions are terms

term_kind includes profession, and terms has a nullable parent_id. Nine profession rows: Riding coaches, plus the eight horse care professions. A profession inherits everything terms already do: aliases (shoer, blacksmith, hoof trimmer), slug history and 301s, a photo, SEO title and description, active and inactive. terms also gains featured and featured_order, so the menus are picked in admin.

B

Its words and behaviour live in profession_details

One row per profession, keyed on the term.

  • Identity: door (coaches or horse-care), glyph key, sort order, launch state.
  • Nouns: singular ("farrier"), plural, short label ("Physios"), what their specialities are called ("discipline", "speciality", "service"), who they serve ("rider", "horse owner"), what the years field means ("years shoeing"), the job title used in structured data.
  • Page words: hero headline, lead, short lead, the one-liner, steps, FAQ, the pitch to professionals.
  • Behaviour: enquiry options ("Regular visits", "One-off visit"), events_enabled (on for every profession at launch), remote_allowed (on for coaches and nutritionists, off for farriers and dentists), tier labels (so "Clinic" can read differently for a farrier), and the completeness weights and wording (section 08).

Repeating blocks (steps, FAQ, enquiry options, completeness items) are JSON validated against a fixed shape on save, so a form can grow a fourth step without a migration but can never save something the page cannot draw.

C

Specialities stay the "discipline" kind

Barefoot trimming, remedial shoeing, lameness work-ups: each is a term of kind discipline with parent_id pointing at its profession. The word a visitor sees comes from the profession's noun, so a farrier's page says "specialities" and a coach's says "disciplines", from the same table and the same queries. Skills and setup terms get the same optional parent_id: blank means shared by everyone ("mobile service", "after hours"), set means one profession only ("float loading" for coaches).

D

A profile is not an account

Today a coach profile's id is the user's id, so one login can only ever be one profile. That is the single thing that would make practices a rebuild later. The new model separates three things:

  • providers: the public profile, with its own id. Name, slug, headline, bio, base location and travel radius, remote flag, business name (optional), status, cohort, acquisition_source, invite_id, organisation_id (nullable, unused at launch).
  • provider_members: which users can edit which profile, with a role. At launch every profile has one row, its owner. Security rules check membership, never "is this my id".
  • subscriptions: the Stripe customer, price, tier, status and founding terms, with a billing_owner that points at a provider today and can point at an organisation later.

One plan covers every profession a provider has: someone who coaches and does bodywork is one profile, one subscription, shown in both sections.

E

Room for practices, not built yet

Launch is people only, with an optional business name. When practices come (a vet clinic, a saddlery, a riding school), the additions are: an organisations table (name, slug, logo, address), organisation_members, organisation_id filled in on each person's profile, a practice page listing its people, and a subscription whose billing owner is the organisation, covering several profiles. Nothing written at launch has to change for that, because nothing assumes one user is one profile or that a profile pays for itself. The same shape covers riding schools, the call deferred on 7 September.

F

Status and review

providers.status is one of draft, in_review, published, hidden or changes_requested, with reviewed_by, reviewed_at and a note. Every public query, security rule, search function and page gate reads status = 'published'; there is no separate published switch to drift out of step.

G

Page copy lives in content_blocks

A key, an optional profession or door it belongs to, a JSON value with a known shape, and who changed it when. Keys like home.hero, door.horse-care.hero, pitch.professionals, email.enquiry, email.monthly.zero, prompt.mention. It gets the settings table's five rules exactly: the default lives in code (so the page never shows blank), one typed reader per block, validation on save, a history row on every change, and a short cache that is cleared the moment an admin saves.

05

Search, pages and addresses

1

Search knows professions

nearby_providers() takes profession ids alongside the discipline, skill and setup arrays: OR within a kind, AND across kinds, as now. Ranking stays distance first with "based in the area" above "travels to it". Remote providers only appear where the profession allows remote, and never on suburb pages. Search never mixes the two doors in one list.

2

The gates count per profession

indexable_pages gains a profession, and its page types become profession plus area, and profession plus speciality plus area. The three-provider gate counts providers of that profession, so ten coaches in Geelong do not switch on an empty Geelong farriers page. The featured rules count the same way: no featured block in an area with fewer than eight providers of that profession, and at most three featured per profession per area.

3

The addresses, all moved before launch

Since nothing is indexed, the route move happens with the rebuild instead of as a separate stage. Every open profession is a top-level section from one template:

  • /coaches, /coaches/[discipline], /coaches/in/[area], /coaches/[discipline]/in/[area]
  • /farriers, /farriers/[speciality], /farriers/in/[area], /farriers/[speciality]/in/[area], and the same for every profession
  • /profile/[slug] for every provider, whatever they do
  • /horse-care stays as the door's index of professions; /horse-care/[slug] 301s to the profession's own section

Old coach addresses get a generated redirect map, provider slugs checked before discipline slugs, as already planned.

4

Reserved words

A profession slug sits at the top level, so one called search or about would take over an existing page. A reserved list lives in code (every top-level route: search, about, admin, account, dashboard, profile, events, login, signup, join, horse-care, for-coaches, for-professionals, api and the rest), the admin form refuses a profession slug on it, and a database check refuses it too.

5

The door comes from the data

Today the horse care colours and the see-through header are switched by lists of paths in an inline script, the header effect and OVERLAY_ROUTES, and adding a profession means adding its path to each. That breaks the no-deploy test. Instead the page's door is worked out on the server from the route and the profession rows, and data-door and data-overlay-route are rendered on <html> with the first byte. The inline script goes, and with it the regex gotcha recorded in CLAUDE.md.

6

Structured data and the SEO digest

A profile's Person markup takes its job title from the profession row and knowsAbout from its specialities; a provider with a business name also gets LocalBusiness. Every profession's events get Event markup. The weekly digest groups Search Console pages by the URL patterns of every open profession, reads them from the rows, and splits zero-result searches by profession, so a supply gap says "farriers near Ballarat" rather than just "Ballarat".

06

How a professional signs up

One route for every profession, with the profession decided at the start and everything after it reading that profession's row.

1

Arrive with a profession already known

Every way in carries it: /for-coaches, a new /for-professionals with a profession picker and that profession's pitch, /list-your-business, and Kim's messages, which link to /join/farriers. Each lands on /signup?profession=farriers&plan=listed. The link also carries where they came from (ref and the usual utm tags), which becomes acquisition_source. The current bug where plan links send ?plan= and the form reads ?tier= is fixed here.

2

Make the account

Name, email, password, and the profession shown with a "change" link rather than a question. The account is a provider, and a providers row plus its owner membership are created together. Confirming the email lands them in onboarding, not the dashboard.

3

Five short screens of onboarding

  • What you do: the profession (with "I also do…" for a second one, covered by the same plan), then that profession's specialities, with the suggested ones ticked first.
  • Where you work: base suburb, how far you travel, whether owners come to you, remote or online work where the profession allows it, and a business name if you trade under one.
  • You: photo, a one-line headline, a few sentences in your own words, qualifications, registration and insurance, all shown as "supplied by".
  • How owners reach you: the enquiry form, phone and email, each behind click-to-reveal, as coaches have now.
  • Your plan: the founding offer while it is open (section 09), otherwise the three plans with that profession's labels.

Every screen saves as it goes, so leaving halfway leaves a draft, not a lost form. The labels and help text on every screen come from the profession row: a vet is asked about "services", a coach about "disciplines", from the same form.

4

Preview, then submit for review

The last screen shows the profile as an owner will see it. Submitting needs the finished-profile minimum: a photo, a bio, one speciality and a location. It goes to in_review, and the provider sees "we'll have a look and let you know, usually within a day or two".

5

Review, with an alert

Every new profile is looked at before it goes live. The moment one is submitted, an email goes to every address in the review_alert_emails setting (Alana and Kim to start) with the name, profession, area and a one-click link to it in the review queue. From there it is publish, or ask for changes with a note the provider sees. Publishing sends the provider an email saying where they now appear, which keeps the fallback-ladder promise: every published provider is on at least one live page.

Review is a setting, review_required, on at launch. Turned off, a finished profile publishes itself and the alert becomes a "new provider" notice instead, so you still know who joined. Later edits to a published profile go live straight away and are not re-reviewed; a changed photo or name appears in a "recent changes" list in admin.

6

Kim's invites

The rule stands: never publish a profile somebody has not agreed to. In admin Kim enters a name, email and profession, optionally starts the profile from what she already knows, and sends a link from her own message. The link opens the sign-up with everything filled in; nothing is public until the professional finishes onboarding, submits, and passes review. Each invite records Kim as the source, which answers the "which outreach converted" question at month eight.

No custom fields

Professions differ, and the temptation is a form builder where each profession defines its own fields. Every real difference so far fits the existing shapes instead: what they do is specialities, how they work is setup terms with an optional one-line detail, the rest is nouns and labels on the profession row. A form builder would be the single most expensive part of this plan and would make search and area pages much harder. Revisit only if a profession genuinely needs data that fits none of those.

07

Riders, across every profession

The stated goal is riders and owners coming back, whether for a coach or a farrier. One site only pays off if a rider who signs up once hears about everything relevant to them. Today the rider side only knows about coaching events.

1

Alerts for any profession

rider_alerts replaces the single preferences row. A rider can hold several alerts, each with a place and radius, a door or specific professions, optional disciplines or specialities, and what to hear about: new events, a new provider near them, or both. "Tell me when a barefoot trimmer starts working near Kyneton" is one row.

2

Event reach is a distance

An event is promoted to riders whose alerts match it and who are within reach. Listed and Spotlight events reach riders within their own alert radius; Clinic tier events are pushed further, to event_reach_km (a setting, 250 km to start), so a Toowoomba workshop reaches the Darling Downs rather than Cairns.

3

One account page, one monthly email

Saved providers work for any profession. "Coming up near you" on the account page draws from every profession the rider follows. The monthly rider email is one email from the brand, with sections for each door the rider cares about: new events, new providers near them, and the occasional note that a profession has opened in their area.

4

Consent, stored properly

Each alert records when and how the rider opted in, every email carries a working one-click unsubscribe, and unsubscribing is honoured at once. That is the Spam Act, and it also keeps the list, the business's most valuable asset, clean enough to be worth something.

08

Proving value to every provider

The month-eight conversion rests on showing a provider what the listing did for them. All of that was written for coaches, and a farrier who gets a coach-shaped report will not trust it.

1

Every measurement carries the profession

provider_events (impressions, views, reveals, enquiries), search_events and provider_month_stats all record the profession, so a provider in two sections sees what each one did for them, and a benchmark compares a farrier with farriers, never with coaches.

2

The monthly numbers email in their words

The good-month and zero-month versions are content blocks with the profession's nouns as variables: "owners" rather than "riders" for horse care, "visits" rather than "lessons". The outcome question, the local search terms and the one profile gap work the same for everyone.

3

Completeness per profession

The score out of 100 keeps its shape, but the items and weights live on the profession row: a coach is asked for a photo of them coaching, a farrier for a photo at work, a vet for their registration. The dashboard's "one thing to do this month" reads from it.

4

The mention prompt

"When you get in touch, let Jane know you found her on Equine Professionals Australia." It is a content block, uses the provider's first name, and says the brand, never a section.

5

The dashboard

The same tabs for everyone (overview, enquiries, profile, events, billing), with labels from the profession row. A provider in two professions sees both on one dashboard, with the stats split by section.

The founding offer hangs off the launch date, which does not exist yet. Three settings, all in admin:

  • launch_date: empty until launch day, when it is set once and then locked. It is the date every founding member's free period is counted from.
  • founding_free_months: 6. The first charge for a founding member is this many months after launch_date.
  • founding_join_by: sign up before this to qualify. Separate from the free period, so the offer can close to new sign-ups without shortening it for the people already on it.

A card is taken at sign-up and the sign-up says, in plain words next to the card field, that nothing is charged until six months after launch. Before launch day the wording is "six months after we launch, and we'll email you the exact date on launch day"; from launch day it is the date itself. On launch day every founding member gets that email with their first charge date, and the usual reminders go out 30, 14 and 3 days before it. Technically: the card is saved without a subscription (Stripe setup mode) while there is no launch date; locking the date creates each founding member's subscription with its trial ending on the first charge date; after launch, sign-ups get the subscription and its trial straight away.

A provider's cohort is recorded as founding or open at sign-up and never changes, so the month-eight question "did the founding offer convert?" can be answered per profession.

The test

Add a profession in admin, say equine chiropractors, fill in its words, set it live, and nothing else. /chiropractors, its area pages, the Horse care menu and door colours, the footer, the sitemap, the search select, rider alerts, the monthly emails and the SEO digest all include it, with no deploy. If any of them needs a code change, this plan is not finished.

Deliberately left in code:

  • The doors and their colours. Two doors, each with a checked-contrast colour pair. A third door is a real design decision and should cost a deploy.
  • The glyph library. Drawn to one grid and weight. Admin picks one per profession; a new drawing is a small code change, which keeps them consistent.
  • Layouts and templates. Fields, not page building.
  • Gate logic and ranking. The numbers are settings; the rules that use them are not.
  • The reserved URL list. It is a list of the site's own routes, so it changes when the routes do.
  • The handbook. Planning documents stay in git, dated, as a record.

Terms of service

The existing plan for the terms says we do not vet or accredit coaches. It now has to cover vets, dentists and physiotherapists, some of whom are registered with a state board. Registration numbers and insurance are shown as supplied by the provider, never as checked by us, and the terms say so in plain words. This is a job for the solicitor, alongside the partnership agreement.
