Handbook Equine Professionals Australia

One site, two front doors

Coaches and every other equine profession on one site, under one name. The recommendation on sites, names and domains, then the routes, redirects, navigation and database work that follow. Settled 23 September: Equine Professionals Australia, at equineprofessionals.com.au, registered. Section seven is how the coaching section keeps a clear identity of its own, and how every profession after it gets the same treatment without a redesign.

Site structure September 2026 Decision plus build spec Sections 1 to 3 are the decision
01

The recommendation

One website. One domain. One database. One rider account. Two front doors. There is no site two. There is a coaching section, and a horse care section that holds every other profession, starting with farriers. Each profession behind the second door gets its own pages without anyone touching the design.

That was already the plan for the build. What is new is that both professions launch at once, which turns two decisions that were comfortably in the future into decisions for this month: the name at the top of the site, and the domain it lives on.

Four ways to run two professions
OptionWhat happens to searchUpkeepVerdict

The search argument is the loudest one, and it is worth being precise about why. Ranking for "farrier Geelong" is not about the words in the domain. It comes from having a real page about farriers in Geelong, internal links pointing at it from pages Google already trusts, and a site that does not look like a thin directory. A subfolder gets all three on the first day it exists. A second domain gets none of them and has to start the six to twelve month climb from scratch, at the same time as the coaching side is still climbing.

The second argument matters just as much and gets forgotten. The rider list is the asset. One site means one login, one saved list, one set of alert preferences, and one monthly email that can say a farrier has started taking clients twenty minutes away. Two sites means the rider signs up twice, and you own half a list twice.

Bonus

This also dissolves the open question about what to call site two. Sections do not get names registered anywhere. What needs a name is the business and the domain, and that is section two.

What everyone else does

Researched 22 September 2026, because it is worth knowing whether this is a solved problem before deciding it from first principles. It is close to solved, and the answer is not the one you would guess from looking at the biggest Australian marketplaces.

What decides it

Audience overlap, not the size of each category. Every business in the list that runs one brand does so because the same person buys across the categories. Every business that runs separate brands is a classifieds business where the buyers do not overlap at all: nobody shopping for a hatchback is also shopping for a combine harvester, and a first home buyer is not buying a warehouse.

ACM said it out loud when AgTrader took on property. Craig Chapman, their agriculture commercial director: "Ninety-six per cent of visitors to AgTrader are farmers, so adding property will provide the perfect platform for sellers and agents to increase their exposure to the people most likely to be interested in buying."

That is this business exactly. The rider who needs a coach is the same person who needs a farrier, often for the same horse in the same month. On the test the whole industry uses, this is a one brand business, and it is not a close call.

Two things worth noting against the grain. Separate brands are not a cheap hedge: SEEK spent around A$180 million to keep three names alive on one platform, which is the price of brand equity that already exists and is worth protecting. Nothing here has any brand equity yet, so there is nothing to protect. And the direction of travel in services is consistently toward fewer brands, not more, with Angie's List, Oneflare and DogVacay all folded into something else rather than kept.

One correction to the premise. I could not find evidence that Horse Deals ever ran separate brands and merged them. An archived version of its old site from years ago already carried horses, transport, property and stallions at stud under the one name, so as far as the record shows it has always been one brand with categories. What changed recently is the site, not the brand structure.

02

The name

Settled 23 September

Equine Professionals Australia, at equineprofessionals.com.au and equineprofessionals.au. One business name, one domain. The partnership, its ABN and TFN and the business name were registered on 23 September 2026. The IP Australia trade mark search on the exact string is the one check still outstanding; the nearest existing name is Equine Professional Hub Pty Ltd in Queensland. Equine rather than Equestrian because a farrier or a dentist serves the horse, not the rider, and because it does not read as an arm of Equestrian Australia.

Two rules that come with a descriptive name. The wordmark is the words, never the initials: EPA is the Environment Protection Authority in every state. And a descriptive name cannot be protected by registration alone, so the site has to be the one everybody uses, which is a marketing fact rather than a legal one. The section below is the reasoning that ran before the decision, kept because the trade-offs are still true.

Short version, as it stood before the decision: Equestrian Coaches Australia is the wrong name for the whole site, and there is no reason left to keep it for anything. It has not been said to a single coach, so there is no recognition to protect, and a second name in circulation for one site is a cost with no return. It retires. The coaching section is called Coaches, and section seven is about how that section gets an identity of its own without a second name.

The reason is not search. It is what happens on the page. A rider who lands on a page of farriers in Geelong and reads "Equestrian Coaches Australia" across the top thinks they have the wrong site and leaves. A farrier that Kim is trying to recruit reads a web address that says the site is for coaches and needs the discrepancy explained before anything else gets discussed. That happens on every page of half the site, on both sides of the marketplace.

So the site needs a name above both sections. The name currently written down for that is Equestrian Professionals Australia, and it fails both tests this handbook already set for a parent name. It describes a category, which makes it close to impossible to protect at IP Australia and easy for a competitor to sit next to. And it is forgettable, which is the one thing the parent name cannot afford to be, because remembering it two years later is its entire job.

Those were not new objections. Before we start says it in the section on what to avoid, and it lists twenty-odd alternatives that all survive the sentence "find a farrier on ...". That list is still the right place to choose from.

If you want a recommendation rather than a shortlist

The evidence in section one also settles a worry about going wide: a single name really does hold unrelated categories. Horse Deals covers horses, floats, property and saddlery under one word that means none of them, and EquiDirectory covers 47 categories. A wide name does not have to be a vague one.

Whichever it is, nothing gets bought until all three checks come back clean: an ASIC business name search, an IP Australia trade mark search on the exact string, and domain availability. The trade mark search is the one that can actually stop you, and someone else's registered mark beats your business name no matter who registered first.

One thing to hold onto from the old name. The parent name does not need to carry the search words, because page titles do the ranking. "Dressage coaches in Geelong" is the title, the parent name is the suffix, and a coach or a farrier reading a cold message from Kim gets the path instead of a second brand: the address ends in /coaches or /farriers, which explains itself just as well and teaches the site's name at the same time.

03

The domain

Launch on the parent domain, and buy only that one.

This reverses what this handbook said on 9 September, so it is worth being straight about why. The old recommendation rested on one sentence: do not pick the parent name yet, because there may never be a second profession to hang it on, and checkpoint three at month eight could say stop. That sentence is no longer true. The second profession is launching with the first. The reason for deferring has gone, and the cost it was deferring, one domain migration, now lands exactly where the old text warned it would, in the middle of launching a new profession.

The argument for a separate coaches domain was that Kim's cold outreach is better with an address that explains itself. It still is, and the path does that job: a link ending in /coaches tells a coach exactly where they are landing, and it shows them the site covers more than coaches, which is a reason for riders to be there and therefore a reason for the coach to list. A second domain would only have given a rider two addresses for one site, and no way to know that the place they found a coach is the place to find a bodyworker.

Nothing has been bought and no business name is registered, so there is no sunk cost in either direction. That will not be true for long, which is what makes this the week to decide it.

What this costs

Less than the September plan. One domain at about $30 a year and one ASIC business name at $108 for three years, instead of two of each. Against it: one guaranteed migration avoided, and one address for riders instead of two.

The rider retention argument that was correctly demoted in September stays demoted. Riders do search again rather than typing a domain from memory, and the mailing list is the channel that actually brings them back. It is not carrying this decision. The page level legibility problem in section two is.

04

Addresses

The build rule has always been that nothing which will eventually hold every profession gets the word "coach" in its address. Profiles currently sit at /coaches/[slug], which breaks that rule, because every profession needs a profile page. It has not mattered yet. It matters the moment a farrier needs one.

Two segments need thinking about. The first is where profiles live. The second is how a section page tells a place from a speciality.

Why the in segment

/coaches/dressage and /coaches/geelong are the same shape, so the code has to guess from a database lookup which one it is holding. That works until a place and a speciality share a word, and then it silently serves the wrong page. /coaches/in/geelong can only ever be a place. It costs one extra segment, reads like English, and Google is indifferent.

The profile move is the only change here that costs real work, and it is free this week and a migration after launch. Nothing is indexed, Search Console is not verified yet, and no domain exists, so today these are file renames and a redirect map rather than a ranking event.

05

Redirects

Only old paths redirect now. There is one domain, so there is no domain-level redirect to set up, and sections never redirect because sections are real pages.

1

One domain, verified once

Verify the domain property in Search Console the day it exists, even with no coaches on the site yet. It only collects from that day forward and only keeps sixteen months, and the twenty-query rank basket in the marketing plan needs the history.

2

Old paths to new ones, one to one

Five rules: profiles to /profile/[slug], /disciplines to /coaches, a discipline page to /coaches/[discipline], a discipline and area page to /coaches/[discipline]/in/[area], and /riding-instructors/[area] to /coaches/in/[area]. Permanent redirects, not temporary ones, and never dumped on the homepage.

3

Generated from the database, not typed out

One script walks providers, professions, terms and areas and emits the pairs. Write it now, while there are a handful of rows to test it against, because it is also the script a domain move would need later.

Trap

After the move, /coaches/something is both a discipline page and the old profile address. The middleware has to check the provider slugs first and redirect those to /profile/, then fall through to the discipline lookup. Get that order wrong and a coach called Jane Western breaks a discipline page.

06

Navigation

With two professions the navigation stays flat. At three or more it needs one grouped menu, and it is worth building the header so that change is a data change rather than a rewrite.

07

One brand, two front doors

The brief from Alana and Kim: one name and one domain, the coaching section has to stand out clearly and be able to stand on its own, and the design must not be a coaches and farriers design. Farriers are one profession of many. Bodyworkers, dentists, saddle fitters and vets follow, and the system has to take each of them as a row in a table, not a design job.

The first version of this section got that wrong by treating each profession as a section. This version fixes it with one idea. The top level of the site is not the profession. It is who the client is. A rider is either looking for someone for themselves, which is a coach, or for someone for their horse, which is everyone else. That is the whole top level, it never grows past two, and every profession fits under one of the two doors.

Two levels, and what identity lives at each
LevelWhat it isIdentity it carries
DoorExactly two. Coaches, and Horse care. Who the client is. Coaches serve the rider. Horse care serves the horse: farriers, bodyworkers, dentists, saddle fitters, vets, and whatever comes next. The accent pair, the front-door landing page, the header tab, the search-card control, the pitch page. Fixed in code; a third door is a deliberate migration and a new accent pair, which is the right amount of friction.
ProfessionAs many as you like. A row on terms with a door. Riding coaches today under the first door; farriers today under the second, then the rest. The glyph, its own landing page, its nouns and copy, its photographs, its area and speciality pages, its pitch copy block. Never its own colour.
Why colour stops at the door

Six professions with six colours is a rainbow, and a rainbow is what a site looks like when nobody decided anything. The Guardian caps its section colours at five for the same reason. Here the colour says which door you are behind and the glyph says which profession you are looking at. Farriers and dentists share the steel and sky pair and differ by a horseshoe and a tooth. A rider never has to learn a palette.

The principle underneath is unchanged. The brand is the frame; the door is the picture; the profession is the caption. Everything structural is shared and identical: header and footer, type, radii, motion, the search card, result cards, the map, the dashboard, the rider account, the email sender. The door changes the colour and the front page. The profession changes the words, the glyph and the photographs. That is enough for a coach to feel the site was built for coaches, enough for a farrier to find a farriers page rather than a page about horse care in general, and little enough that the twelfth profession costs nothing but its row.

1

Two real front doors, then a page per profession

/coaches is the first door and, while coaching is the only profession behind it, also the coaching landing page: its own rise headline, promise line, search card scoped to coaches, real counts, its own photograph, the founding offer, and one "List your coaching profile" button. A coach arriving from Kim's message has seen a complete coaching site. /horse-care is the second door: a hero, the search card with the profession select, and one tile per profession with its glyph and a real count, honest about the new ones. Behind it, /farriers is a profession page from one template, with the door's accent and its own glyph, copy and photo. When a dentist row appears, /dentists renders with no code change. That is the test the build has to pass.

The homepage is the switchboard: the brand, one search card with the two door control, and a block per door.

2

One accent pair per door, set by one attribute

Coaches keep terracotta #B4553A on cream and peach #e8b79a on ink. Horse care gets steel #3F6480 on cream and sky #A9C4DA on ink. Measured on the real grounds: steel on cream 5.6:1, cream on steel 5.6:1, sky on deep ink 8.6:1, the same job peach does at 8.6:1. Blue against orange stays distinct under the common colour blindness. The layout sets data-door on the html element for anything under a door or a profession, the way Phase 18 sets data-overlay-route, and the stylesheet swaps the accent tokens off it. Accent appears in italic emphasis, eyebrows, primary buttons, chips, map pins, badges and focus rings, never on the brand mark, the footer or body text.

3

A glyph per profession, a brand mark that is not initials

One simple line glyph per profession in currentColor, so it takes the door's accent: helmet, horseshoe, tooth, saddle, a stethoscope for vets, and so on as rows arrive. Used in the Horse care menu, on badges, on result cards and as the map pin. It has to read at 16 pixels, which is the constraint that keeps them simple.

The brand mark is still to be rebuilt, because the current wordmark carries the retired name. Never the initials; EPA is a state regulator. The arch is the suggestion: already the photographic signature, scales to a favicon, belongs to nobody. Full wordmark in Instrument Serif on desktop, "Equine Professionals" on phones.

4

The words come from the profession row

A coach teaches disciplines to riders and runs clinics. A farrier has specialities, serves horse owners and takes bookings. A dentist has services and a recall interval. Those nouns are data: plural, tagline, hero title, hero copy, the word for what they specialise in, the word for who they serve, the verb on the call to action, the image, the glyph, and the door. Professions are already terms, and terms already carry description, image and SEO fields from the discipline editor, so this is a handful of nullable columns and a second tab on the same admin screen.

Do not invent a profession's pitch. The coaching pitch is honest because it came from the plan. Each new profession's pitch block gets written after the first ten conversations with that profession. Until then the generic horse care pitch says what is true for all of them: owners search here, you get an email when one gets in touch, here is what it costs.

5

Same photographs, different subject

Same golden-hour grade, same arch crop, same rule that no horse shows stress. Coaches are rider and coach together in the arena at the end of the day. Horse care is hands and the horse: a farrier at a hoof, a bodyworker's hands on a back, a dentist with a gag and a head torch. One photographic language, the subject says the profession. The budgeted half-day shoots coaches and at least one horse care profession at the same property.

How it holds together when someone moves around

The header carries the brand mark and the two door tabs, the active one in its accent. The Horse care tab opens a menu of professions with glyphs and counts, and it is the same menu at two professions or twelve. Inside a door the tab stays lit and the accent stays on. Switching doors keeps the town the rider typed. The search card never mixes professions. A profile page takes the accent of its primary profession's door and shows every profession badge the person has, so someone who coaches and does bodywork is one page with two badges in two colours, which is honest. Emails come from the brand with the door's accent and the profession's glyph. The dashboard is identical for everyone with labels read from the row.

Page titles keep the search word in the body and the brand as the suffix: "Dressage coaches in Geelong | Equine Professionals Australia", "Farriers in Geelong | Equine Professionals Australia", "Equine dentists in Geelong | Equine Professionals Australia". Breadcrumbs show the door: Home, Horse care, Farriers, Geelong.

What this is not

It is not two brands, and it is not one brand per profession. No section logo, no section domain, no section social account, no section email address, and no profession gets its own colour. Those are the things that make a section feel like a separate company to a rider, and they are the things that cost two of everything to keep alive.

08

The database

This is the part that turns out to be almost free, because the taxonomy was built for it. How riders find us chose one terms table with a kind column on the grounds that a fourth kind should be a row rather than a migration. That bet pays off here.

1

Profession becomes a fourth kind of term

Add profession to term_kind, and a nullable parent_id on terms so a discipline or speciality knows which profession it belongs to. Professions have no parent and generate pages. Seed one profession row for coaching, backfill every existing discipline's parent to it, then add the farrier row and its specialities.

2

A provider's profession needs no new column

coach_terms already links a provider to any number of terms with a sort order, and professions are now terms. So a provider's professions are simply their profession rows, and the lowest sort order is the primary one, exactly the rule already used for their primary discipline. Somebody who coaches and does bodywork is two rows, which is the honest answer.

3

Search takes one more argument

nearby_coaches already accepts discipline, skill and attribute arrays plus a state. It gains a profession, and is dropped and recreated because the signature changes, the same way the state parameter was added.

4

The page register gains a profession

indexable_pages gets a profession column, and its two page types become profession and area, and profession, term and area. The nightly recompute joins the profession through coach_terms. The three provider minimum stays exactly as it is, and it will keep most farrier pages switched off for months, which is correct.

5

Aliases matter more here than they did for coaching

Riders do not say farrier consistently. They say shoer, blacksmith, barefoot trimmer, hoof trimmer. Equine dentist gets searched far more than equine dental technician. The alias layer already exists and the admin screen already edits it, so this is seed data rather than code.

Migration note

Two enums grow here, and alter type ... add value cannot run inside a transaction, which is what the migration runner sends. Run both statements out of band first, the same way the three tier values were added, then run the file.

Two things deliberately not changed. coach_profiles keeps its name even though it now holds farriers, because renaming it touches every row level security policy, every embed, and the foreign key disambiguation that is already documented as a trap, and no rider ever sees it. And the three tiers stay as they are for both professions at launch. The top tier is called Clinic, which makes less sense for a farrier, so make the label come from settings per profession rather than adding a fourth tier.
