The promise

Nobody pays for exposure twice

Every directory a coach has ever been in sold them "exposure", took the money, and then went quiet. That is the wall this page has to get over, and no amount of feature bullets does it.

What gets over it is the one thing we can say and they can't:

The line the page should lead with

Most directories take your money and show you nothing. We email you every month with how many riders saw your profile, how many clicked to call, and how many got in touch — including the months when the answer is none.

That is already built. The events table, the click-to-reveal buttons and the enquiry form all exist to make a coach's month countable, and the monthly email has an honest zero-month version as well as a good one. It is the most valuable thing we have and it should be the headline of the page rather than a line in a comparison table.

The second thing worth saying loudly: we never take a commission on a lesson. Booking platforms take 10 to 20% of every booking. We take a flat monthly fee and nothing else, forever. It costs us nothing to promise and it is a real difference.

The offer

Three tiers

Travel radius is not a tier. Every coach sets how far they travel, on every plan. Driving to your students is the normal case rather than an upgrade, and some coaches teach by video, where distance means nothing at all. The one place distance does scale is how far a clinic gets promoted.

Everything a coach needs to be found and contacted is in Listed. The two tiers above it sell reach and information, never a better position in the organic results.

For most coaches

$9.99 /month

Listed

A real profile, a working contact button, your full travel radius, and the monthly numbers email. Everything needed to be found and hired.

This is the tier we expect most coaches to sit on, and it has to be good enough that they never feel short-changed.

For coaches building a book

A featured slot on the area pages, alerts the moment an enquiry lands, and the search data behind the numbers — which queries you appeared for, and where you sat.

For the coach with room in the diary who wants to fill it.

For coaches who are already full

$49.95 /month

Clinic

Events pushed to riders by email across the state, event pages Google can put in its results, and a waitlist that captures the riders you can't take yet.

Not more of Spotlight. A different job for a different coach.

Two build rules that come with an untiered radius

Rank by distance, don't cap it. If radius is free on every plan, nothing stops a coach setting 300 km and landing on forty area pages they will never drive to. The answer is not a cap to police. It is ordering: a coach based in the area sits above a coach who travels to it, and the card says which. Riders get the right answer and nobody has to argue about a number.

Remote coaches don't go on suburb pages. A coach teaching by video belongs on the discipline pages, on a dedicated online-lessons page, and in national searches. Putting them on all 400 area pages would undo the three-coach gate and hand Google exactly the thin-page pattern it spent 2024 demoting.

Where the 40% comes from

After Stripe we keep $9.52, $24.21 and $48.78. On an expected mix of 70% Listed, 25% Spotlight and 5% Clinic, the average coach is worth $15.16 a month instead of $10.74. A pessimistic mix of 85/13/2 still gives $12.22; an optimistic 60/30/10 gives $17.85.

At fourteen months' average stay that lifts what a coach is worth over their life from $153 to about $212, which raises the most we can afford to spend signing one from $50 to roughly $70. That matters more than the monthly number, because it is what decides which recruiting channels are affordable.

Moving between tiers

Coaches change plan themselves, whenever they like, both directions. A coach running a clinic in March goes up to Clinic for March and back down in April. That is the intended use rather than a loophole, and it is why there is no separate one-off event product to build or explain.

Annual is ten months' price — $99, $249 and $499 — which is the usual reason to pay up front and helps a business with no cash. A coach on annual who wants a clinic month upgrades and pays the difference for the remainder of the term.

The pitch

What we actually do to bring riders to you

This is the section the page is missing, and it is more persuasive than the tier table. A coach does not want to know what a feature is called. They want to know what happens after they pay, and who does it.

The other side

What riders get, and why it matters to coaches

None of the coach-facing promises work unless riders keep coming back. Horse Deals gets people returning 7.8 times a month, which is classifieds behaviour — people checking for what's new. A directory that never changes does not earn that.

Three things bring riders back, and each one is worth explaining on the coach page because each one is a coach's traffic.

  • →
    A monthly rider emailWhat's on near you, new coaches in your area, and one useful thing to read. This is the durable asset — Equestrian Hub has 15,000 subscribers against 10,000 monthly website visitors, so in this market the list is bigger than the traffic.
  • →
    Events near meA rider sets their area once. When a clinic is posted within reach, they hear about it. This is the single strongest reason for a rider to open the email rather than delete it, which is why events are free to list on every tier and only the promotion is paid.
  • →
    A saved search that tells them when someone new joinsA rider looking for a liberty coach in an area with none today gets told the day one appears. Cheap to build, and it turns an empty search — our worst moment — into a future visit.
The conflict worth naming

Putting events behind the top tier would starve the thing that brings riders back. At launch almost nobody will be on Clinic, so almost no events would exist, and the feature never gets going.

So listing an event is free on every tier and the distribution is what's paid for. We want the maximum number of events on the site for rider traffic; coaches pay for reach, not for permission.

The proof

Clicks that aren't invisible

The reason a coach cancels is not that the listing was expensive. It is that they had no idea whether it did anything. Every number below already has somewhere to come from in the build.

The monthly email is the part that does the work, because it arrives whether or not they log in. This is roughly what it should look like.

The zero-month version of that email is the one that matters more. It says so plainly, gives the same numbers, and tells them the two things most likely to fix it. A coach who gets an honest zero month and a suggestion trusts the next email. A coach who gets silence cancels.

Why the search number is on the paid tiers

Profile views, reveals and enquiries go to everyone, because those are the proof that the listing works and the reason anyone upgrades. Search impressions and the queries behind them are the Spotlight feature, because they answer a different question: not "did it work" but "why not, and what would fix it".

That data comes from Google Search Console, is free to us, and starts producing numbers before the site has meaningful traffic — which makes it the strongest thing we can show a coach in the first six months.

One decision

Testimonials are never capped

The obvious move is to cap them on the cheap tier. It is the wrong one, for three reasons.

They are the thing that makes us rank. A profile with eight rider testimonials is content that exists nowhere else on the internet, and it is the main reason our area pages can beat a templated competitor. Capping them on the tier that holds most of our coaches means taxing the exact content that makes the site work.

They are the thing that converts a rider. A parent choosing a coach for their child reads the testimonials and nothing else. Fewer of them means fewer enquiries, which means a coach with a worse month, which means a cancellation.

It reads as mean. In a community this small, "you may only display three of your happy students" is the kind of thing that gets screenshotted.

Sell the tools instead

Unlimited testimonials on every tier. Spotlight buys the one-click request link, the automatic reminder in the monthly email, the ability to pin a favourite quote to the top of the profile, and a right of reply.

Same revenue, none of the damage — and it pushes coaches to collect more of the content we need rather than fewer.

The line

Four things we don't sell

Worth agreeing now and writing on the page, because each one is a temptation that would make money next month and cost the business later.

  • 01
    We never sell a place in the organic resultsA featured slot is a labelled block above the list, capped at three per area, rotating evenly between the coaches who bought it. The list underneath is ordered on relevance and nothing else. Sell the ordering and the value of featured collapses as more coaches buy it, and riders stop getting the best match — which is the only thing that makes the site worth visiting.
  • 02
    We don't sell featured in areas with fewer than eight coachesFeaturing one of three is absurd. The coach will feel cheated and ask for their money back, and they'll be right. Where the area is thin, Spotlight still buys everything else and we say so honestly at signup.
  • 03
    We never say "verified", "accredited" or "recommended"Unless we have genuinely checked, which we haven't. Coaches can upload a qualification or a certificate of currency and we'll display it as something they supplied. A real checked badge is possible later as a paid add-on, but it's Kim's time, not a tier feature.
  • 04
    We never take a percentage of a lessonA flat monthly fee and nothing else. This is the sharpest difference between us and every booking platform, and it should be said in large type on the page.
The launch

Free first, then $9.99

The founding group is free so the directory has something on it, and free is also the only offer Kim can make at volume before there are riders to point at. Then it becomes a paid site, because at ten dollars it should be.

The risk in that plan is the moment the free period ends. It is the same cliff as before, and it is still the single biggest test the business faces.

Three things that soften it

Land them on Listed, not on the bill. Founding coaches get Spotlight free for the founding period. When it ends they drop to Listed at $9.99 rather than being asked to choose between $24.95 and leaving. The step down is small and the decision is easy.

Lock the rate. Founding coaches keep $9.99 for as long as they stay, even after the price rises for everyone else. It costs nothing today and it is the reason they'll say yes to Kim now.

Let the numbers do the asking. The three conversion emails lead with their figures, not our pricing. A coach who has watched eleven enquiries arrive over six months is not really being asked to start paying; they're being asked to keep something.

Still unresolved from the marketing plan

The founding coaches were promised twelve months free but the paid checkpoint sits at month eight. Both can't be true. The recommendation has not changed: make it six months free with the rate locked forever, and find out whether coaches will pay while there is still time and money to change course.

Not settled

What is still open, and what we're watching

Most of the offer is now decided. Three things genuinely aren't, and two more are decided but need checking against real behaviour rather than argument.

Open

  • Kim has not seen $24.95 and $49.95$9.99 for Listed is settled. The two prices above it are a first proposal and nobody has tested them on a coach. Worth putting into the first ten conversations the same way as the entry price: don't lead with a number, ask what they would expect to pay for a listing that filled a clinic, and let them answer first.
  • Six months free or twelveCarried over from the marketing plan and still unresolved. The founding coaches were promised twelve, but the paid checkpoint sits at month eight. Recommendation unchanged: six, with the rate locked forever.
  • How wide "statewide" is for a clinic pushA rider in Cairns does not want a clinic in Toowoomba, and Western Australia is a state you can't drive across in a day. Probably a distance rather than a state boundary, but it needs a number before the rider email goes out.

Decided, but being measured

  • Indicative pricing is optional and trackedRiders want it badly and plenty of coaches won't publish it, so we don't enforce it. We record who fills it in, then compare enquiries per profile view between the coaches who show a price and the ones who don't. If it lifts enquiries we can tell coaches that with a number in month six, which is a far better conversation than pushing it now.
  • Riding schools list individual coaches, or get in touchNo separate product for now. The likely behaviour is that a school creates one coach profile and fans enquiries out to whoever is free — we can't stop that and it isn't worth stopping. What we can do is notice it: a profile taking far more enquiries than its peers, or a bio naming several instructors, is the signal that there's demand for a venue account worth building.
Off the table, for a reason worth remembering

A "responds within 24 hours" badge would be excellent and we can't measure it. Enquiries go straight to the coach's own phone and inbox, so we never see the reply. It only becomes possible if messages route through the platform, which is a year-two question and a real trade-off — routing them adds friction to the exact thing we most want to happen.

One thing the page can't have yet

Coach testimonials, because there are no coaches. Don't invent them and don't leave an empty section where they'll go. Use the founding offer as the close until the first five founding coaches can be asked for a line each — then it becomes the strongest block on the page.
