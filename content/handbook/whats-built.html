01

How to try things

Everything here is on the test site, the password-protected copy of the whole site. Alana has the address and the password. The public address still shows only the coming soon page.

To see the professional side, you need a professional login. Alana keeps a test coach account (Tess Coachman, in Bendigo, on Spotlight) and a test rider account, and can give you the logins. Or sign up yourself with any email you can open.

Words you change in Admin can take a minute to show. Reload after that.

We’re building first and going back to test and polish after, so if something looks rough, note it and we’ll pick it up in that pass.

02

Opening to professionals before riders

What it is. A middle step between the coming soon page and the full launch. When we switch it on, coaches and horse care professionals can join, set up their profile and pay on the real address, while riders and horse owners still see the coming soon page. That’s also what Stripe needs to see before it will switch on real payments.

The coming soon page gets a box inviting professionals to list their business. Their pages show a simpler menu with only what’s open. Nothing new shows up in Google until the full launch.

It’s built but not switched on: it’s one setting on Vercel, flipped when we’re ready for the first coaches (after the database move to Sydney and the Stripe account).

03

Extra locations on the Clinic plan

What it is. Some professionals work from more than one place: a coach who teaches at home and at an agistment centre an hour away, a farrier with two regular rounds. On the Clinic plan they can add up to two more places, so three in all, each with its own travel distance.

Riders searching near any of those places find them, and the result shows the nearest one (someone searching near Ballarat sees “Ballarat”, not the main base). Each place counts toward its own area page. The profile says “also works from” the other places.

On a smaller plan the extra places are kept but don’t show, and they come back if the professional moves up again. How many places each plan allows is a number in Admin, Plans and prices.

Try it

Ask Alana to put the test coach on Clinic, then open Dashboard, Profile and scroll to “Other locations”. Add a town and look at the profile preview.

04

Testimonials

What it is. Testimonials are the quotes a professional chooses to show on their profile. They’re labelled “Provided by the business” and never count toward the star rating. Reviews are different: riders write those, every genuine one goes up, and professionals can’t pick or pin them. That split is deliberate, and it’s what the ACCC expects.

On every plan, a professional can add testimonials themselves, edit their own, put them in order, hide them and delete them, as many as they like. There’s a new Testimonials tab on the dashboard for it.

On Spotlight and Clinic they also get:

  • A request link to send a client. The client writes it in their own words, adds their name and town, and ticks that it can be shown. It waits on the dashboard until the professional chooses to show it, and they get an email when one arrives. A client’s words can’t be edited.
  • Pin a favourite to the top of their profile.

Reminders. The monthly numbers email now has a line with their testimonial and review counts and the link to ask for more, until they have five. The dashboard also suggests asking once enquiries start coming in.

Which plans get the link and pinning is a tick box in Admin, Plans and prices (“Testimonial tools”). Moving down a plan never deletes anything.

Try it

Log in as the test coach and open Dashboard, Testimonials. Add one, then copy the request link, open it in a private window and write one as if you were a rider. Back in the dashboard, show it and pin it. Then look at the profile with ?preview=1 on the end of the address.

05

Photos and intro videos

Photos upload properly now. Big phone photos used to fail on the live site because of a size limit on the way through our server. Photos now go straight to storage, which fixes it for profile photos and every photo upload in Admin. Storage refuses anything over 25MB or that isn’t an image.

Intro videos are YouTube or Vimeo links instead of uploaded files, as the plan for Spotlight always said. The professional pastes a link (unlisted videos work too). Their profile shows a still with a play button, and nothing loads from YouTube or Vimeo until a visitor presses play, which keeps our “no tracking” promise.

Try it

As the test coach: Dashboard, Profile. Add a photo from your phone, then paste a YouTube link into the Intro video box and press play on the preview.

06

Legal pages

The terms of service and privacy policy are now draft 2, and there are two new pages: Cookies (the whole list of what the site stores, which is very little) and Cancelling and refunds (how billing, cancelling, pausing and refunds work). The review policy has two new sections: what happens when someone says a review is false, and the rules on incentives. Competition terms have a proper “Your details” section and the fine print.

All of them still say “draft” at the top and stay out of Google until the solicitor has checked them and we switch “legal approved” on in Admin, Settings. The prices, ABN and dates in them fill themselves in from settings, and the words can be edited in Admin, Pages.

Every form that takes someone’s details now has a short line saying who gets them and why, with a link to the privacy policy: the enquiry form, both sign-ups, reviews, competitions, guides and the new testimonial page. The plans have a line about billing and cancelling, with a link to the refunds page.

Marketing emails now carry the business name, ABN and Buln Buln, Victoria, and say why the person is getting them (“you asked for ... on [date]”). The tick-box wording on the forms is the new version from the legal draft.

Stripe needs to see these pages without a password, so the legal pages open on the public address even before launch, and the coming soon page links to them.

Try it

Read through /terms, /privacy, /cookies and /refunds. They’re linked at the bottom of every page.

07

The For Professionals page

/for-professionals is now as complete as /for-coaches: the honest monthly numbers promise, the founding offer, what we do to bring horse owners to them, the plans side by side, “we never take a percentage of a job”, and questions. It’s in the horse care steel blue, written for horse owners and jobs rather than riders and lessons. Both pages share the same building blocks, so a change to the plans shows on both.

Arriving with a profession in the address (/for-professionals?p=farriers) makes the page about farriers and sends every button straight to farrier sign-up.

08

Smaller changes

  • Suburb boxes only take a real suburb. Anywhere a professional, rider or admin saves a place (onboarding, the profile, extra locations, alerts, the “hear when” box, testimonials and the admin screens), you now type, pick from a list of real Australian suburbs, and it fills in with the state and postcode and a tick. Nothing saves until one is picked, so no more misspelt towns or the wrong Richmond. The horse care search box suggests towns too, but search still takes whatever you type. Try it: sign up as a test coach and type “Richmond”.
  • Emails have their own dark mode. With dark mode on, the Outlook app was turning our cream emails a flat grey. Every email now has a deep green version for phones and computers set to dark, with cream text and the terracotta button. Gmail still picks its own colours. Try it: switch your phone to dark mode and sign up on the test site.
  • Nobody is named on the site. Following the tone audit, the site talks as “we” to everyone. The About page is now five short parts with no bios or photos, the coaching page no longer names who answers the Facebook posts, and the terms and privacy policy give the business name and ABN only. The “Site by Daiquiri Creative” credit stays.
  • The iPhone status bar matches the photo. On newer iPhones the strip behind the clock and battery showed grey above the big photos at the top of the home, coaches and horse care pages. It now shows the photo itself behind the clock, and once you scroll, the cream header and the strip above it are one colour.
  • Checked on phones, page by page. Every page was tested at phone and tablet sizes, logged out and in. Fixed: iPhones zooming in when you tap a box to type, footer links too small for a thumb, the enquiry button on profiles squashing onto two lines, a floating Map button covering the email box on empty searches, and a couple of pages that ran off the side of a small screen.
  • The test site can open without a password. Once it’s switched on, anyone with the link can look around test.equineprofessionals.com.au. Google still won’t list it. Plan changes are switched off there while it’s open, so nobody can give themselves a free plan; everything else works as before.
  • The database now lives in Sydney. Everything moved across from the old one in Tokyo (logins, words, settings and photos), so it’s where the privacy policy says it is. Nobody needs a new password.
  • A safety lock on wiping the database. The tool that rebuilds the database from scratch now refuses to run once a real professional has joined, so the move to Sydney can’t be repeated by accident on live data.
  • Friendly error pages, and we hear about errors. If a page breaks, people now see a plain “this page didn’t load” page with a Try again button, a way home and our email, instead of a technical error. Every error is also reported to a service called Sentry, which emails us, so we know before a professional has to tell us. It’s set up and working.
  • Nothing looks frozen any more. Buttons show a spinner while they’re working, logging in keeps spinning until the dashboard appears, and a thin bar runs across the top of the screen while a page loads.
  • The footer is tidier: the policy links, the ABN and Buln Buln, and “Site by Daiquiri Creative”.
  • The menus in the header have a cleaner drop-down bar, and “Search all” is in bold.
  • Riders now see “Close my account” on their account page.
