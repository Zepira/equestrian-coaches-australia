Metadata

Title tag — About Equestrian Coaches Australia

Meta description — A rider and a coach built a directory for Australian riding coaches, because finding the right one shouldn't come down to luck.

H1 — About us

Finding a riding coach in Australia still runs on word of mouth. You ask at a competition, you ask in a Facebook group, you ask the person whose horse goes the way you'd like yours to go. It works, eventually, if you already know people. If you're new, or you've moved, or you want something more specific than general ridden work, it mostly doesn't.

We built this so that finding a coach is a search, not a stroke of luck.

H2 — Why we built it

Alana found her coach by accident. Kim's posts turned up on Instagram, and that was it — no directory, no search, no recommendation. There are very few coaches in Australia teaching what Kim teaches, and there was no way to go looking for one on purpose.

That's the whole idea. Somewhere a rider is looking for a liberty coach, or a Western coach, or a dressage coach who works with nervous riders, and somewhere near them is exactly that person, and neither of them will ever hear about the other. A directory is an unglamorous fix for that, but it is the fix.

H2 — The rider

First person, Alana.

I came to horses late. My first lesson was at fifteen, which in pony-club terms is close to a mature-age student, and I bought my first horse at eighteen.

My mare now is Rosie, a grey Australian Andalusian who would much rather learn a trick than do another twenty-metre circle. She is clever, opinionated and genuinely willing, which is a combination that doesn't let you get away with anything. Most of what I know about working with a horse rather than at one, I know because she made it clear when I'd asked badly.

I build software for a living. So I built this.

H2 — The coach

First person, Kim. Needs her sign-off before it goes live.

I coach foundational connection, lightness and body feel — the things that sit underneath every discipline, whether you're jumping, doing dressage or riding bridleless in an open paddock.

I've been working with horses for [X] years and teaching for [X] of them, in Australia and [countries]. I work with youngsters through to advanced horses, and with riders from their first lessons through to competition. My specialties are liberty, bridleless riding, classical dressage, showjumping and the transition to bitless, and a lot of what I teach is confidence, biomechanics and mindset as much as it is technique.

What I'm actually after, in all of it, is harmony — a horse and rider who understand each other well enough that the aids stop being instructions and start being a conversation. Bridleless riding is the clearest test of that, but it isn't the point. The point is the partnership that makes it possible.

H2 — How we do things

We don't take a commission. Coaches pay a flat monthly fee to be listed. What you agree with your coach is between you and your coach, and we never see a cent of it.

We don't vet coaches, and we'd rather say that plainly. We check that a listing belongs to a real person who agreed to be listed. We don't inspect facilities, verify qualifications or assess teaching. Ask the questions you'd ask anyone you were about to get on a horse for.

Nobody gets listed without saying yes. We don't scrape profiles or build pages about coaches who haven't heard of us.

We tell coaches the truth about their numbers. Every listed coach can see how many riders saw their profile and how many got in touch — including when the answer is none. A directory that hides that is selling hope.

We don't sell anyone's details. Not riders', not coaches'.

H2 — Who we are, for the record

We're Alana and Kim, based in south-east Victoria, and we're in the process of registering as a partnership. Kim coaches, and she's listed on this site like any other coach — she gets no ranking advantage, no editorial preference and no discount. If that ever changes, we'll say so here.

H2 — We're new, and we'd rather say so

This site launched in [month/year] and the coach list is still filling out. If there's nobody in your area yet, that's because we haven't reached them — not because they don't exist. Tell us who's missing, or tell your coach we're here.

Why the disclosure paragraph is in there

Kim is a listed coach on a site she co-owns. That is fine, and it is much better said on the About page than discovered by a coach paying $24.95 a month. It is the same promise as “we tell coaches the truth about their numbers”, pointed at ourselves.

Two decisions

Things to settle before this goes live

Decision one — where “animal communicator” sits

This is a positioning choice, not a detail. It will land strongly with part of the liberty and ethical-horsemanship audience, and it will cost credibility with part of the competition dressage and showjumping audience. The whole pitch of this site is that it serves every discipline.

Recommendation: leave it off the shared About page and put it on Kim's coach profile, where riders searching for exactly that will find it. The About page's job is to explain the business; a full specialisation list there makes the site look like Kim's shopfront. Kim makes the call — but deliberately, not by copy-paste.

Decision two — the photographs

Both images are credited to Lisa Gordon / Little More Grace Photographics and one carries a visible watermark. Get written permission before either goes on the site. A watermarked photo on a commercial page is both a licensing exposure and a bad look. A short email asking for permission and terms is usually enough, and photographers generally say yes when they're credited.
