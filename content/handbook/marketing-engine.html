Handbook Equine Professionals Australia

The marketing engine

What marketing this business will actually run, what a WordPress site would have got from plugins, which outside services are worth paying for, what Australian law makes the software do, and the marketing tools we build into the site and the admin to support all of it, in the order to build them.

Research and build plan, revision 1 25 September 2026 Builds on The Site as a CMS Open decisions, section 11
01

The short version

We don't need a WordPress-style marketing suite. We need about ten small tools, and most of them run on their own once built. The market is a few thousand professionals and a rider list we hope reaches the low thousands in year one. Nobody in Australian equestrian buys Meta ads, and two founders on a few hours a week can't run campaigns by hand every week. So the marketing that works here is the kind the site does by itself: emails that go out when something happens, tools that let professionals promote themselves (and us with them), and pages that turn a visitor into someone on the list.

Most of the hard parts already exist. The site has rider alerts, the monthly numbers email, the rider round-up, tracked invite links, the SEO digest, featured slots and an admin that edits every email. What's missing is on the email marketing side: a proper consent record, one list of everyone we're allowed to email, a way to send one-off campaigns to a chosen group, sequences that stop when they've done their job, and the share tools for professionals.

What we buy: almost nothing. Resend already sends our email and is cheap to grow on. Stripe already does promo codes. The one real new cost is Vercel Pro at about A$30 a month, which we need anyway: Vercel's free Hobby plan is for non-commercial use only, and this is a business. By the end of year one, at around 5,000 people on the list, the whole marketing stack is about A$125 a month.

What we build, in order: the consent and list foundations before launch (the law makes these non-negotiable), then the professional share kit, review requests and referrals in the first month, then campaigns, sequences and guides by month three, and the founding-offer conversion campaign well before the first founding members are due to pay.

Found along the way

"Physiotherapist" and "chiropractor" are protected titles under the national health practitioner law, and using them for animal work without registration may breach that law. Our profession names are "Physiotherapists" and "Chiropractors". That needs fixing before launch whatever happens to marketing (section 06). Also: EquiDirectory already lists 917 riding coaches and 844 horse care services, and Equestrian Australia runs a free Coach Finder. Both are competitors the marketing plan should name.

04

Build or buy

The deciding rule: the list lives in our database, in Sydney, and outside tools get only what they need to do one job. The rider list is the most valuable thing the business builds. None of the email platforms checked store data in Australia, so every one of them is an overseas processor the privacy policy has to name. Keeping the master list in Supabase means we can change email provider in an afternoon.

05

What the law makes the software do

This is research from the regulators' own pages (ACMA, the OAIC, the ACCC, the state gaming regulators and the vet and health practitioner boards), with law firm summaries where a page couldn't be read. It is not legal advice, and the questions at the end of this section should go to the solicitor with the partnership agreement.

The risk that matters most is the Spam Act. ACMA fined Oneflare, a services directory, $75,600 in 2019 for texting tradies whose numbers it took from other directories with no unsubscribe. In March 2026 it fined Lululemon $702,900 for putting promotional content in order emails that had no unsubscribe. In a community this small, the reputational cost of one complaint would hurt us more than the fine.

1

A consent record for every person, kept forever

Under the Spam Act the sender has to prove consent. For each person and channel we keep: the type of consent (they ticked a box; they're a paying customer; their business address was published), when, where (the form, invite link or admin user), the exact wording they saw, and when and how they withdrew. Rows are only ever added, never edited. Marketing boxes are unticked by default and separate for each purpose. Signing up for an account or sending an enquiry is not, by itself, consent to marketing.

2

Every email template is labelled factual or commercial

ACMA's position is that any promotional content at all makes a message commercial, even inside a receipt. So each email content block carries a class. A factual email (password reset, enquiry forwarded, receipt) is refused on save if it contains an upgrade line, a referral prompt or a "see what's new" banner. Commercial emails can't be sent without the consent check, the unsubscribe link and the sender details. Rider alerts are commercial: they promote other businesses' services. Treat the monthly numbers email as commercial too, since it will want to mention upgrades; it costs us an unsubscribe link.

3

One unsubscribe list that every send checks

One click, no login, free, honoured at once (the legal limit is five working days), working for at least 30 days after the email went. Rider emails already do this. It has to cover everything: waitlist, rider alerts and round-ups, provider marketing, campaigns, sequences and Kim's invites. Bounces and spam complaints from Resend's webhooks go on the same list. We never email someone who unsubscribed to ask them back.

4

Say who we are in every message

Every commercial email footer carries Equine Professionals Australia, the ABN, and a contact address that works for at least 30 days after sending.

5

Kim's invites need a record of where the address came from

Emailing a professional who hasn't opted in is allowed only when their business address was published (their website, their Facebook business page), nothing there says "no marketing", and the message is about their trade. The invite form records the page it came from and the date. It checks the unsubscribe list, carries the footer, and allows one reminder at most. Personal numbers found in Facebook comments don't qualify.

6

Referral invites are sent by people, not by us

If our system emails someone because a friend typed in their address, we're the sender and the friend's say-so isn't consent. So referrals and "recommend my farrier" work by giving the person a link to send themselves, by text, WhatsApp or email.

7

Reviews: publish the bad ones too

We're a review platform under the ACCC's guidance. Every genuine review is published, including negative ones. A public moderation policy says what gets removed (fake, abusive, defamatory, off-topic, conflict of interest), never "negative" and never "the provider asked". Who removed what, and why, is logged. Paying for a plan has no effect on which reviews show, their order or the average. Any incentive to review has to apply whatever the rating and be shown on the review. Nobody edits review text. Provider-entered testimonials are labelled "provided by the business" and kept out of the average.

8

Paid placements are labelled where people see them

Featured cards say "Featured, paid placement" on the card itself and sit above the list, not mixed into it (already the rule). A "How the list is ordered" page, linked from results, says paying doesn't change organic order. Sponsored content, when it comes, is labelled "Sponsored".

9

Subscriptions: remind before charging, cancel online

New unfair trading practices law passed in 2026 and starts on 1 July 2027. Its subscription rules cover small business customers, so our providers are covered. It requires a clear summary before sign-up, notices before a free period converts and before renewal, and online cancellation. We already plan most of it (the 30, 14 and 3 day founding reminders, the Stripe billing portal). Add an annual-renewal reminder and make sure "free" never appears without the date and the price that follows. Prices show including GST as one figure. No fake countdowns.

10

Privacy: build to the full standard even if we're exempt

Businesses under $3 million turnover are usually exempt from the Australian Privacy Principles. That exemption is lost by a business that discloses personal information for a benefit, unless it has each person's consent. A directory arguably does that, so we keep it only by having consent for everything we publish or pass on. The new statutory privacy tort (since June 2025) applies whatever our size. From 10 December 2026 privacy policies must describe automated decisions that significantly affect people, which could include our ranking and any automatic hiding of profiles. There's no Australian cookie-consent law. But the OAIC found in June 2026 that a generic cookie banner doesn't cover Meta or Google tracking pixels, so keep to our own server-side analytics and add no pixels. The privacy policy names Resend, Stripe and any other overseas processor.

11

Competitions: judged on skill, or a small prize pool

A competition judged on merit ("best photo of your horse with your coach", against published criteria) needs no permit anywhere. A random draw needs a permit in the ACT over $3,000 in total prizes, in South Australia from $5,001, and in New South Wales over $10,000 (the NSW figure needs confirming). Keep draws at $3,000 or less. Full terms are published before entry, entering is free, and entering does not sign anyone up to marketing unless they tick a separate box.

12

Protected titles for physios, chiros and vet specialists

The national health practitioner law doesn't regulate work on animals, but "physiotherapist" and "chiropractor" remain protected titles. Someone unregistered calling themselves an "equine physio" may breach it. "Specialist" is restricted for vets. So: rename those two professions ("Equine physiotherapy and rehab", "Equine chiropractic and manual therapy", or similar; the slugs can stay), allow the protected titles on a profile only with a registration number that's been checked against the public register, record the check date, and block "specialist" in vet titles unless a specialist registration is recorded. No Australian vet board found bans testimonials, but they must not mislead or compare one vet unfavourably with another.

For the solicitor

1. Is the monthly numbers email, with no promotional content in it, a factual message? (We treat it as commercial either way.) 2. Does the directory model count as trading in personal information, and do our consent flows keep the small business exemption? 3. The wording of the founding-offer conversion notice and renewal reminders, once the 2027 regulations are published. 4. The current NSW trade promotion threshold. 5. Whether the Children's Online Privacy Code, due by 10 December 2026, reaches a directory that junior riders might use. 6. Vet advertising rules in New South Wales and Queensland, which weren't fully checked.

06

What we build

Eleven modules. Each follows the CMS rule: the shape is code, the words and numbers are rows editable in admin. Sizes are rough: S is a day or two of build, M a few days, L a week or more.

M1

Audience and consent · M · before launch

The foundation everything else sends through.

  • contacts: one row per email address across riders, providers, the waitlist and invitees, linked to a profile where there is one. Admin sees one person, not three tables.
  • consents: append-only; contact, channel, purpose (rider_alerts, rider_news, provider_news, waitlist, invite), type (express, existing customer, published address), wording_id, source, IP, time, withdrawn time and method. rider_alerts.consented_at and the waitlist move onto it.
  • consent_wordings: the exact text of every checkbox and label, versioned. Forms read their wording from here, so changing the words makes a new version automatically.
  • suppressions: one list, per channel, checked by one function (canSend(contact, purpose)) that every send path has to call. Resend bounce and complaint webhooks write to it.
  • Preference page at /email-preferences, reached from every email by token, no login: each topic on or off, plus "stop everything". It grows out of today's /unsubscribe.
  • Message class on every email content block (factual or commercial), a check on save, and the footer and List-Unsubscribe headers added automatically to every commercial send.
  • The monthly numbers email gets a provider opt-in on sign-up and an unsubscribe that stops the marketing parts, not account emails.
M2

Tracked links and attribution · S · before launch

  • links: a short /go/[slug] for every channel (each Facebook group, each partner, each poster, each PR piece). It logs the click in provider_events' sibling link_clicks, adds the utm tags and redirects. Admin makes one in a line and downloads its QR code.
  • First touch and last touch stored on every sign-up, riders included (today only providers carry acquisition_source).
  • An admin report: sign-ups, finished profiles, paid conversions and riders by source and by month. It answers the month-eight question "which outreach worked?"
M3

On-site capture · S to M · before launch

  • Announcement bar: a content block with message, link, start and end dates, audience (everyone, riders, logged-out, one door) and a dismiss that remembers. The founding offer deadline goes here with its real date, never a rolling timer.
  • Subscribe card: one component, placed on area pages, profession pages, events and guides, that knows where it is ("Tell me when a new farrier starts near Kyneton"). It writes an alert plus a consent row.
  • Empty results: when a search finds nobody, offer the alert and "Know someone good? Send them this link".
  • One slide-in, off by default: shows after a delay, never to someone logged in or already subscribed, never on a profile mid-enquiry, at most once a fortnight. Offers area alerts or a guide.
  • Campaign landing pages at /p/[slug] from content blocks (a Pony Club page, an Equitana page), each with its own tracked link.
  • The waitlist becomes contacts with consents on launch day, and gets the launch email.
M4

Professional share kit · M · launch month

A "Promote yourself" tab on the dashboard. Everything carries the professional's own tracked link, so their dashboard shows how many people each piece brought.

  • Share images generated for every profile, event and area page (the social preview), and a square and a story-sized version to download: photo, name, profession, town, "Find me on Equine Professionals Australia". A "Founding member" version at launch.
  • Printables: an A4 poster with a QR code for noticeboards, a review card to hand out, a business-card-sized QR. Generated as PDFs.
  • Badge for a website: a small image link, plus an optional live review widget once they have reviews.
  • Email signature snippet to copy.
  • Follow link: "Get my clinic dates by email". A rider who opens it signs up (one screen, consent box) and gets an alert on that provider's events. This is a new kind of rider alert: one provider, events only. It's the most important thing in this module.
M5

Rider reviews · M to L · launch month or soon after

  • reviews: rider, provider, profession, rating, text, how it came (after an enquiry through us, or from the provider's request link), status, and the provider's public reply. Separate from testimonials, which stay labelled "provided by the business".
  • Request link in the share kit that a professional sends to every client. The page asks neutrally ("How was your experience with Jane?"), never "tell us why you loved her".
  • Our own follow-up a set number of weeks after an enquiry (a setting): "Did you end up booking Jane?" Yes leads to a review; the answer also feeds the enquiry outcome that the monthly numbers email reports.
  • Moderation queue in admin with reasons from the published policy only, a log of every decision, simple duplicate checks (same email, same device, pasted text) and a "report this review" link on every review.
  • A review tag says "Enquired through Equine Professionals Australia" where true. Never "verified".
  • Review and AggregateRating markup on profiles, following Google's current rules for third-party reviews.
M6

Referrals and promo codes · S to M · launch month

  • Every provider gets a referral link and code. A colleague who joins through it gets a free month (a Stripe coupon); when that colleague's first payment goes through, the referrer gets a month's credit on their Stripe balance. Amounts, caps and the "first payment" rule are settings. Self-referrals are blocked.
  • A "Invite a colleague" panel on the dashboard showing who joined and what they've earned, with full terms on the page ("I get a free month if you join" is shown in the message they send).
  • Promo codes screen in admin over Stripe promotion codes: partner codes (Pony Club members get three months of Spotlight), expiry, limits, first-time only, and redemptions by code. Codes can ride on a tracked link so they apply automatically.
  • Riders refer by sharing: share buttons on events, profiles and guides with the rider's own ref. No rewards, since riders pay nothing.
M7

Emails that go out over days or weeks after something happens, and stop when the job is done. The triggers and exit rules are code; the words, delays and on/off switch are admin. Each step is an email content block, so the existing email editor and test-send already work.

One-off emails to a chosen group: the spring clinic season, a new profession opening in an area, news for professionals, the launch.

  • Audiences: saved filters built from dropdowns, never typed SQL. Riders or providers; door; profession; within so many kilometres of a place; plan; cohort; source; joined between dates; opened or clicked recently. The screen shows the count live, after consent and suppressions are taken off.
  • Composer: subject, preview line, and a short stack of fixed section types (text, a button, events near the reader, new providers near the reader, a guide, a sponsor slot later). Fields, not drag and drop, rendered with the same email template as everything else.
  • Preview as a named person, send a test, schedule, then send in batches with the consent check on every recipient.
  • Results per campaign from Resend's webhooks: delivered, bounced, clicked, unsubscribed, and sign-ups or upgrades from its links. Opens are shown but flagged as unreliable, since Apple Mail inflates them.
  • A public archive of rider newsletters is optional, and cheap SEO content if we want it.

Tables: audiences, campaigns, campaign_sends (one row per recipient, which is also the record of who got what).

M9

Guides · M · by month three

  • guides: title, slug, summary, body in Markdown, hero photo, profession and area tags, author, optional co-author who is a listed professional (their photo and a link to their profile), status and publish date, SEO fields. Edited in admin, with a preview.
  • Pages at /guides/[slug] with Article markup, a related-professionals block by tag and place, and the subscribe card.
  • A "lead magnet" option: a guide can have a PDF download (the annual horse care calendar) delivered by email after sign-up.
  • The weekly SEO digest reports guides alongside the other pages.
M10

Competitions · S · later

A competition record with every required term (promoter and ABN, who can enter, dates, how it's judged, the prize and its value, how winners are told), an entry form with an optional separate marketing box, entry logging with duplicate checks, the judging record, and the winner published. Skill-judged by default. The form refuses a random draw with total prizes over $3,000. Entrants must be 18 or over, or entered by a parent.

M11

Sponsors · S to M · later

sponsors and sponsor_slots: brand, image, link, where it shows (rider newsletter, a guide, an area or profession page), start and end dates. Every slot says "Sponsored", never sits inside a list of professionals, and never uses rider data. Clicks go through /go/, and admin produces a monthly report for the sponsor. Sold by hand as fixed monthly packages.
