# R1: Marketing activities for a two-sided local-services directory, and how well each fits EPA

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Scope: every marketing activity peers run on the **supply side** (paying providers) and the **demand side** (free riders/owners), with evidence, founder time and a fit rating for Equine Professionals Australia (EPA).

**Fit rating key.** Each rating assumes EPA's constraints: a small national market, 1–5 founder hours a week each, no Meta ads, community-led growth through Kim, and the hard rules in the brief (no scraped or unclaimed profiles, no selling ranking or data, no "verified" label without checks, Spam Act compliance, honest numbers).
**Software key.** **[SW]** means the activity needs on-site software support. **[SW-built]** means the brief says it is already built or planned. **[Manual]** means a person has to do it. **[Hybrid]** means a person does it with light tooling.
**Evidence caveat.** Where a number comes from a vendor blog or a platform's own marketing, it is marked *(vendor claim)*. I have not invented any statistics. Where I could not find public data, I say so.

## 0. Frameworks worth knowing (context for everything below)

- **Most marketplaces rely on one or two acquisition levers early.** Lenny Rachitsky surveyed 17 marketplaces. For initial supply, 60% used direct sales, 33% used referral programs, 33% piggybacked on existing networks, and about 25% used word of mouth or subsidies. "The median company relied on just two levers." https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace-911
- **Lenny's "28 ways to grow supply".** Rated high impact: the value proposition on the site, entry points to it, host referrals, direct sales, reaching critical mass, and building trust through reviews ("reviews transform trust when hosts exceed 10 reviews"). Rated small for supply: SEO, broadcast ads and re-engagement calls. https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/28-ways-to-grow-supply-in-a-marketplace (also at https://andrewchen.com/grow-marketplace-supply/)
- **NFX "19 tactics" for the chicken-or-egg problem.** The ones relevant to EPA:
  - get the hardest side first
  - target a tight niche, then repeat
  - build one side as an email list (Craigslist)
  - host meetups and gatherings (Poshmark Parties)
  - build a SaaS tool for one side (OpenTable)
  - apply a geographic constraint
  - apply a time constraint

  NFX also lists "make supply look bigger with automation" (Yelp). **EPA's hard rules forbid that one.** https://www.nfx.com/post/19-marketplace-tactics-for-overcoming-the-chicken-or-egg-problem
- **Atomic network (Andrew Chen).** This is "the smallest network needed that can stand on its own". For EPA, that means one region, one or two professions, and a few dozen providers with enough riders to generate enquiries. Build one, then repeat. https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/atomic-network
- **Growth loops (Reforge).** Each cohort of users should produce the next. The Pinterest loop is: user content → search engines → new users. https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops. EPA's two natural loops are:
  - **Provider-share loop.** A provider shares their profile or badge, their clients land on EPA, some of those riders browse other professions or join the list.
  - **Content/SEO loop.** Provider profiles, events and reviews produce indexable area pages, search traffic arrives, and riders join the email list.
- **Competitive landscape facts found in this research:**
  - **Horse Deals.** The ACM media kit claims 135,000 average monthly users and 2.5M pageviews, 146k Facebook and 20.8k Instagram followers, and 1,500+ magazine stockists. Display ads run at $20–30 CPM, sponsored content from $799, and a newsletter MREC costs $199. https://acm.media/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Horse-Deals-Media-Kit-2025.pdf. The brief's figure of about 46k is monthly *uniques* and may be a different metric; I did not reconcile the two.
  - **Equestrian Hub.** Claims 15,000+ newsletter subscribers, 10,000+ monthly web visitors, 50,000+ podcast downloads and a 500+ member Facebook group. Sells magazine, banner, podcast, social, newsletter and sponsored-content inventory. https://equestrianhub.com.au/advertise.html
  - **EquiDirectory.** Calls itself "the largest online directory for equestrian businesses across Australia and NZ". Its category counts include "EA Riding Coaches/Instructors (917 listings)" and "Horse/Rider Care Services (844)". It has featured listings, a newsletter, an app, events and classifieds. https://www.equidirectory.com.au/ **This is a direct incumbent.**
  - **Equestrian Australia** runs a free **Coach Finder**, and state branches run "Find a Coach" pages. https://www.equestrian.org.au/content/coach-finder, https://www.sa.equestrian.org.au/content/find-coach
  - **Pony Club Australia** has "almost 30,000 members" and "over 750 clubs". https://ponyclubaustralia.com.au/about-us/
  - **Oneflare closed.** Airtasker retired the AU services directory on 30 June 2026 to "focus solely on Airtasker". https://support.airtasker.com/hc/en-au/articles/59294413002393-Oneflare-has-retired-here-s-what-you-need-to-know. This is a reminder that pay-per-lead directories carry a lot of provider resentment, which supports EPA's flat-fee, no-commission positioning.

## 15. Compliance and trust guardrails that shape the marketing

- **Spam Act.** Every rider or provider marketing email needs express consent, or inferred consent from an existing relationship directly related to the message. It must also carry accurate sender identification and a working unsubscribe honoured within 5 working days. https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam. What this means for EPA:
  - Tick-box consent on all forms and giveaways.
  - Separate transactional emails (enquiry, billing) from marketing emails.
  - A "recommend a provider" email to a non-member needs care (§8).
- **Reviews.** Follow ACCC practice (§5.3): neutral asks, no sentiment-linked incentives, and all ratings shown.
- **SEO pages.** Stay clear of doorway and scaled-content abuse. Keep the 3+ provider gating and add unique local content (§6.1).
- **Trade promotions.** Giveaways follow each state's permit rules; the NSW threshold is $10k (§6.9).
- **"Verified".** Badges should describe facts ("EA accreditation checked on [date]", "5+ reviews"), never imply unchecked verification.
